# Design Charter

## Product statement

**The Neverwildens** is a persistent, text-first fantasy economy RPG. The player begins in debt with a damaged home and one pet creature. They can build wealth through trade, property, information, auctions, resource rights, racing, betting, exploration and risk. The player fights personally in classic turn-based RPG combat; owned creatures are raised, valued, raced, bred and entered into creature competitions.

The world initially feels like strange fantasy. Beneath it sits evidence of lost material systems, disconnected routes and physical "gods" whose surviving components are treated as magic. This hidden cosmology grows from the Material Deities concept without immediately explaining the fantasy away.

## Emotional promise

The game should provide four simultaneous pleasures:

1. **Economic cleverness:** noticing value before other people do.
2. **Attachment:** caring about a home, pets, collections and recurring characters.
3. **Discovery:** learning how the world and its old systems actually work.
4. **Consequence:** seeing decisions alter later opportunities, prices and relationships.

The surface can be warm, funny and domestic. Its history and institutions can be unsettling. Target tone: roughly 60% wonder and 40% dread.

## Locked priority order

1. Trading and economic growth
2. Creature ownership and collection
3. Choices and persistent consequences
4. Property ownership and dealing
5. Exploration and world discovery
6. Player combat and modular magic
7. Gathering systems
8. Racing as a management/simulation discipline

Priority does not mean racing is unimportant. It means economic and creature systems must work before racing receives specialised depth.

## Central player fantasy

The player is an indebted nobody who may become a merchant prince, landlord, collector, breeder, racing owner, scholar, explorer, fighter, faction powerbroker or some overlapping combination.

There is no single mandatory career and no one final victory. Progress is expressed through accumulated capability, assets and world impact.

## Structure and time

- The save is persistent rather than a disposable 30-day run.
- Chapters and seasons create deadlines, obligations and scoring moments.
- The opening chapter uses debt pressure to teach the economy.
- A normal day has three local action points.
- Trading, inventory organisation and ordinary buying/selling do not consume actions.
- Training, racing, gathering, major social work and combat consume actions.
- Travel consumes one or more whole days and refreshes local actions on arrival.
- Properties pay or cost money on scheduled ticks.
- Racing, auctions, migrations, taxes and faction matters can be seasonal.

## Success measures

### Player legacy

- **Wealth:** cash, inventory, transport, businesses and property minus liabilities.
- **Creature collection:** species breadth, rarity, pedigree, market value and achievements.
- **Knowledge:** maps, research, magic understanding, old-route discoveries and verified information.

### World condition

- **Trade prosperity:** route health, production, movement of goods and settlement activity.
- **Faction balance:** influence and reputation across several competing institutions.

These are not five survival bars. Some are summaries; others actively shape simulation.

## Economy

- Regional base prices are understandable.
- Daily variation and events create opportunity without making prices pure noise.
- Supply, access, seasons and world choices eventually feed prices.
- Information is an asset: rumours, surveys, pedigrees, valuations, maps and tips can be bought, sold, trusted or falsified.
- NPC competitors may buy property, move goods, bid at auction and corner markets.
- Crime is optional and region-specific: smuggling, poaching, fencing, bribery and illegal competition offer exceptional risk/reward.

## Property

Four asset classes:

1. **Personal:** home, estate, storage, library, vault and creature habitats.
2. **Residential:** rent and property speculation.
3. **Commercial:** shops, inns, warehouses and service businesses.
4. **Resource:** mines, fisheries, woodland, farms and development rights.

Management depth varies by property. Residential property can be mostly passive. Businesses and resource properties use light management: upgrades, workers, output, maintenance and strategic decisions rather than daily spreadsheet chores.

Property values respond to prosperity, routes, faction control, population, infrastructure and player choices.

## Creatures

Creatures are pets and owned animals, not the player's combat party.

- The player may own a large collection divided into companions, racers, fighting animals, working creatures and breeding stock.
- One or more pets can be emotionally important without making all creatures irreplaceable.
- Creatures have age, bond, temperament, traits, performance statistics, pedigree and market value.
- Both breeding and individual transformation/development are supported.
- Racing is primarily owner/manager simulation: selection, training, feed, equipment, course fit, bloodline, form and betting.
- Creature fights are also management/simulation events.
- Creature treatment and commodification create meaningful choices without prescribing one moral answer.

## Player combat and magic

- The player fights alone.
- Combat uses strict, readable turns inspired by the classic JRPG era.
- Core commands are Attack, Magic, Item, Defend and Flee.
- Equipment provides socket layouts.
- Magic exists as permanent, tradable physical modules.
- Active and support modules can be linked for combinations.
- Modules grow or unlock mastery through use in later milestones.
- Rare modules create economic dilemmas: equip, collect, research or sell to acquire property.
- Ordinary defeat should cost time, money, condition or opportunity. It should not casually delete a beloved pet or the entire save.

## Skills, perks and traits

- **Skills improve through use.** Trading grows by trading; fishing grows by fishing.
- **Perks are chosen** at skill thresholds and create specialisation.
- **Traits emerge** from repeated behaviour, events and relationships.
- Traits may combine benefit and drawback and are normally persistent until changed through a specific system or story.

## Gathering and crafting

- Fishing and mining use short branching sequences for ordinary activity.
- Rare locations can expand into multi-step stamina, hazard or expedition sequences.
- Gathering feeds trade, property, equipment, creature care and discovery.
- Personal crafting remains minimal or recipe-simple.
- Complex work is commissioned from specialists or performed by owned production properties.

## Choice design

Borrow the strongest part of Reigns: quick decisions, remembered state, recurring characters and delayed effects.

- Choices appear during travel, exploration, business, faction and major property events—not compulsorily every morning.
- Immediate directional effects may be visible; delayed consequences stay uncertain.
- Good choices represent competing priorities rather than an obvious correct answer.
- Recurring characters remember how they were treated.
- A decision can alter prices, routes, laws, property values, factions, creatures or future event decks.

## World model

- Start with one dense region, then expand through newly accessible routes.
- Settlements track prosperity, trade access, security, faction control and property conditions behind the scenes.
- A relatively small cast of persistent named NPCs should appear to live independent lives.
- Time and seasons pass; the protagonist does not initially age out of the game.
- Owned transport—mounts, carts, wagons, boats, flying creatures and old-route access—changes capacity, speed, risk and status.
- Auctions are a signature intersection of property, creatures, relics, information, rivals and wealth.

## Hidden cosmology

The oldest magic may be surviving physical infrastructure, engineered life or material intelligences. The world was once connected more widely and may have been deliberately isolated or quarantined.

Do not reveal this as a cheap "technology all along" twist. Religious, magical and material interpretations can remain simultaneously meaningful. Discoveries must change present-day mechanics: trade, property, routes, creatures, laws and faction power.

## Explicit exclusions for the early game

Do not build these before the core loop is proven:

- multiplayer;
- a player-run global auction house;
- romance, marriage, children or dynastic inheritance;
- hundreds of creatures;
- full-city visual construction placement;
- real-time pet neglect mechanics;
- complicated personal crafting trees;
- a complete simulated national economy;
- prose generated at runtime by an external language model;
- a graphical timing minigame for every activity.
